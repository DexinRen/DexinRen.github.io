## Ingredients

| Name        | Amount    | Notes                                                 |
| ----------- | --------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| White sugar | 100 g     | Refined white sugar preferred                         |
| Water       | 30 g      | Just enough to wet the sugar; no stirring during cook |
| Acid (opt.) | 1–2 drops | Lemon juice or vinegar — prevents crystallization     |
| **Coolean** | _Varied_  | See coolean section below for types and amounts       |
## 🔥 Cooking Procedure
1. Add sugar, water, and acid (if using) to a **small, clean saucepan**.
2. Heat over **medium heat** without stirring.
    - Sugar will dissolve → bubble → color gradually.
3. Watch for desired **color stage** (see color chart below).
4. Once target color is reached, **remove from heat immediately**.
5. **Add the selected coolean** (see below) to stop cooking and stabilize.
    - Always use **hot** cooleans to avoid sugar shock or splatter.

## ❄️ **Coolean Types & Quantities**

| Coolean       | Amount (for 100 g sugar) | Function                                 | Use Case Example               |
| ------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **Hot water** | 50–80 g                  | Neutral quenching and dilution           | General-purpose caramel base   |
| **Hot lard**  | 30–50 g                  | Glaze integration, pork fat synergy      | Pork-based dish                |
| **Hot broth** | 50–100 g                 | Adds umami, integrates caramel into dish | Direct meat braises, stocks    |
| **Hot cream** | 100 g                    | Converts to dessert caramel sauce        | Crème caramel, sweet glaze     |
| **Alcohol**   | 10–30 g                  | Adds aroma & halts caramel sharply       | Gourmet sauces, aromatic glaze |

> ⚠️ Always add coolean **slowly and carefully**. Molten sugar reacts violently to water or fat.

## 🎨 Color Stage Reference

CAUSION, observing the color of a thin layer of the syrup, the larger the syrup's depth, the darker the color. only observe a thin layer of syrup

| Stage        | Temp (°C)  | Flavor Profile          | Visual Cue                 | Recommended Use                    |
| ------------ | ---------- | ----------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Light amber  | ~170 °C    | Sweet, mild             | Pale gold, no browning yet | Transparent syrup, mild glaze      |
| Medium amber | 175–180 °C | Nutty, rich, balanced   | Honey/tea colored          | 樱桃肉, 红烧, general meat dishes       |
| Dark amber   | 185–190 °C | Slight bitterness       | Deep reddish-brown         | Heavy meats, counter-bitter glazes |
| Burnt        | >195 °C    | Acrid, bitter, unusable | Almost black, sharp smell  | ✘ Discard                          |

Light amber taste like Maltose, the more it close to medium to dark amber, the more complex the flavor (BUT Dark Amber is where the bitterness start to generate)

## 🧊 Storage (after quenching)

|Form|Container|Shelf Life|Notes|
|---|---|---|---|
|Liquid caramel|Glass jar|1–2 weeks (fridge)|Store tight-lidded, rewarm to use|
|Dry caramel|Sheet then break|Dry box, room temp|Can be remelted or powdered|
